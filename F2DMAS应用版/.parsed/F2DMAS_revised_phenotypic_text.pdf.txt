10   Abstract


11   Plant phenotyping requires accurate and repeatable three-dimensional structural information, but practical acquisition conditions in

12   greenhouses, seedling rooms, and indoor pot experiments often include complex backgrounds, handheld motion blur, and thin leaf

13   structures. These factors reduce the robustness of conventional three-dimensional reconstruction methods and limit their use in low-cost

14   and automated phenotyping. To address this problem, this paper proposes F2DMAS, an automated three-dimensional plant phenotyping

15   workflow using consumer-grade smartphone videos. The workflow first converts multi-view RGB videos into image sequences and

16   removes motion-blurred frames through frequency-domain quality filtering. A frequency–spatial plant segmentation module, termed

17   FSAM3, is then introduced to separate plant structures from complex backgrounds without task-specific annotated training data. The

18   segmented image sequences are further reconstructed using 2D Gaussian Splatting, followed by TSDF-based meshing, scale recovery,

19   and virtual measurement for extracting plant height, canopy width, leaf length, and leaf width.Experiments were conducted on 15 plant

20   species under two acquisition scenarios. The proposed workflow achieved stable plant reconstruction under non-ideal background

21   conditions, with PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS values of 31.09, 0.9711, and 0.0365, respectively. Compared with the baseline reconstruction

22   workflow, F2DMAS substantially reduced the processing time for mesh extraction while improving reconstruction quality. The extracted

23   phenotypic traits showed strong agreement with manual measurements, with R² values ranging from 0.8999 to 0.9879 and RMSE values

24   ranging from 0.64 to 1.21 cm. These results indicate that F2DMAS can provide an end-to-end workflow from smartphone video

25   acquisition and plant segmentation to three-dimensional reconstruction and phenotypic trait extraction. The proposed method offers a



                                                                            1
26   practical and deployable solution for greenhouse seedling cultivation, potted plant experiments, and low-cost three-dimensional plant

27   phenotyping.


28   Keywords: plant phenotyping; three-dimensional reconstruction; smartphone video; 2D Gaussian Splatting; unstructured environment




29   1. Introduction


30      Plant phenotyping provides fundamental data for crop genetic improvement, cultivation management, and

31   intelligent agricultural decision-making. High-throughput, non-destructive, and high-precision phenotyping

32   methods quantify plant growth processes, canopy structure, stress responses, and varietal differences, thereby

33   supporting crop breeding and production management(Nguyen et al., 2025; Walsh et al., 2024). Traditional

34   phenotypic measurement mainly relies on manual ruler measurement, caliper measurement, and visual

35   recording, which suffer from low efficiency, strong subjectivity, poor repeatability, and possible disturbance to

36   natural plant growth (Zhu et al., 2024). Two-dimensional image analysis improves phenotyping efficiency to

37   some extent, but it remains difficult to accurately characterize three-dimensional structural information such as

38   plant height, canopy width, leaf spatial posture, and canopy volume under canopy self-occlusion, leaf overlap,

39   and complex spatial morphology (S. Li et al., 2025a; Y. Li et al., 2025). Therefore, building a low-cost,

40   repeatable, and automated method for three-dimensional plant phenotyping is an important problem in plant

41   phenotyping research and intelligent agricultural applications (Harandi et al., 2023a).

42      In recent years, three-dimensional sensing and reconstruction techniques, including LiDAR, RGB-D cameras,

43   multi-view photogrammetry, NeRF, and Gaussian Splatting, continue to develop and provide multiple technical

44   routes for three-dimensional plant phenotyping (S. Li et al., 2025b; Y. Li et al., 2025; Rincón et al., 2022).

45   However, when these methods move from laboratory algorithm validation to greenhouses, seedling rooms, and

46   ordinary agricultural research scenarios, they still face clear deployment limitations. On the one hand, LiDAR,

47   multi-camera arrays, and some RGB-D devices can acquire high-quality three-dimensional data, but their

48   hardware cost, acquisition requirements, and data processing barriers limit their adoption in small- and medium-

49   scale breeding trials, potted plant studies, and teaching or research scenarios (Jin et al., 2021; Zhu et al., 2021).


                                                                         2
50   On the other hand, real or semi-real agricultural environments often contain cluttered backgrounds, illumination

51   changes, similar colors between leaves and backgrounds, blur caused by handheld recording, and variations in

52   plant posture, which easily reduce the stability of three-dimensional reconstruction and subsequent phenotypic

53   measurement (Wang et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2024). In addition, many reconstruction pipelines still require

54   manual background cleaning, model repair, scale calibration, and phenotypic measurement, making it difficult

55   to form a continuous automated process from acquisition and reconstruction to measurement output. For

56   practical applications, the key problem is not only whether a visually high-quality three-dimensional model can

57   be obtained, but also whether analyzable phenotypic data can be collected in batches in a low-cost, minimally

58   invasive, and repeatable manner.

59      Existing three-dimensional reconstruction methods have their own advantages in plant scenes, but their

60   application conditions and scope remain limited. SfM-MVS-based photogrammetry relies only on RGB images

61   and has the advantages of low cost and flexible acquisition. However, it depends on stable texture and reliable

62   feature matching, and easily produces missing point clouds, background adhesion, and incomplete

63   reconstruction under weakly textured leaves, severely occluded canopies, and complex backgrounds (S. Li et

64   al., 2025b; Wen et al., 2024). NeRF can synthesize novel-view images with strong visual realism, but its implicit

65   representation is sensitive to density thresholds during mesh extraction, and the stability of geometric

66   boundaries and thin sheet-like structures still struggles to meet the needs of phenotypic measurement (Choi et

67   al., n.d.; Mildenhall et al., 2022; Zheng et al., 2024). 3D Gaussian Splatting improves rendering efficiency

68   through an explicit Gaussian representation, but its volumetric ellipsoid representation may cause surface

69   thickening, boundary expansion, and normal noise when handling thin-walled organs such as leaves, thereby

70   affecting the measurement of leaf scale and canopy structure (Kerbl et al., 2023). In contrast, 2D Gaussian

71   Splatting uses surface-oriented two-dimensional Gaussian primitives, which are more suitable for representing

72   thin sheet-like structures such as plant leaves and also benefit subsequent explicit mesh extraction (Huang et

73   al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024) However, using 2DGS alone still cannot address input quality fluctuations and

74   background interference in complex agricultural scenes. When raw images contain motion blur, background

75   textures, supports, soil, pots, or other non-target regions, the reconstruction process may allocate substantial
                                                            3
 76   representation capacity to irrelevant backgrounds, which increases the difficulty of mesh cleaning and

 77   phenotypic measurement.

 78      The development of vision foundation models provides a new way to reduce the cost of plant image

 79   segmentation and background removal. Foundation models represented by Grounding DINO, the SAM series,

 80   and related vision-language models show strong zero-shot perception ability and can extract target regions

 81   without domain-specific annotated data (Ravi et al., 2024; Singh et al., n.d.; Zhu et al., n.d.). However, general

 82   vision foundation models cannot be directly used as ready-to-use tools in agricultural scenes. Plant images often

 83   involve mutual occlusion among leaves, blurred boundaries of small organs, similar colors between targets and

 84   backgrounds, continuous viewpoint changes, and unstable illumination. As a result, single-frame segmentation

 85   may produce inconsistent multi-view masks, missing targets, or false background segmentation. Meanwhile,

 86   blurred frames generated during handheld video acquisition affect SfM feature matching and the stability of

 87   subsequent Gaussian reconstruction. Therefore, deployable three-dimensional plant phenotyping requires the

 88   systematic integration of image quality filtering, plant segmentation, multi-view reconstruction, meshing, and

 89   scale-aware phenotypic measurement, rather than relying on a single algorithmic module.

 90      To address these problems, this paper proposes F2DMAS, an automated three-dimensional plant

 91   phenotyping workflow for unstructured environments. The framework takes multi-view RGB videos captured

 92   by consumer-grade smartphones as input and sequentially performs video frame extraction, frequency-domain

 93   image quality filtering, plant segmentation driven by vision foundation models, SfM camera pose estimation,

 94   2D Gaussian Splatting-based three-dimensional reconstruction, explicit mesh extraction, scale recovery, and

 95   phenotypic trait measurement. Unlike traditional pipelines that depend on dedicated devices or manual post-

 96   processing, F2DMAS aims to achieve automated processing from smartphone video acquisition to quantifiable

 97   three-dimensional phenotypic output.

 98      The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows. First, this paper builds an automated three-

 99   dimensional plant phenotyping workflow based on consumer-grade smartphone videos, which converts multi-

100   view RGB videos into scale-recovered three-dimensional meshes and phenotypic traits without dedicated

101   imaging hardware or domain-specific annotated data. Second, it integrates frequency-domain frame quality
                                                              4
102   filtering with vision foundation model-based segmentation to form FSAM3, a dual-domain preprocessing

103   module that reduces the effects of handheld motion blur and complex backgrounds on subsequent reconstruction.

104   Third, it combines 2D Gaussian Splatting, TSDF meshing, and scale recovery to support the automatic

105   extraction of three-dimensional plant phenotypic traits, including plant height, canopy width, leaf length, and

106   leaf width. Fourth, it validates the effectiveness of the workflow on a dataset containing 15 plant species and

107   two acquisition scenarios. The experimental results show that F2DMAS has good application feasibility in terms

108   of reconstruction quality, mesh extraction efficiency, and phenotypic measurement agreement.


109   2. Materials and methods


110   2.1. Overall System Architecture


111     F2DMAS is an automated three-dimensional plant phenotyping workflow designed for unstructured

112   environments. It aims to convert multi-view RGB videos captured by consumer-grade smartphones into scale-

113   recovered three-dimensional models and corresponding measurements for phenotypic analysis. The workflow

114   consists of six stages, including data acquisition, image preprocessing, three-dimensional reconstruction,

115   explicit mesh extraction, scale recovery, and phenotypic trait measurement. Together, these stages form a

116   complete processing pipeline from video acquisition to phenotypic output.

117     Recent studies have shown that three-dimensional plant representations can support quantitative phenotyping

118   by capturing geometric traits that are difficult to obtain from two-dimensional images, including plant height,

119   canopy structure, leaf morphology, and organ-level spatial variation (Harandi et al., 2023b). Existing image-

120   based plant reconstruction studies have also demonstrated the feasibility of using multi-view images or image

121   sequences for 3D reconstruction and phenotypic trait extraction(Li et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2025). Therefore,

122   F2DMAS is designed as an application-oriented workflow that connects low-cost video acquisition, image

123   preprocessing, 3D reconstruction, explicit meshing, scale recovery, and phenotypic measurement into a

124   continuous pipeline.



                                                             5
125      In the data acquisition stage, a consumer-grade smartphone is used to capture multi-view videos around

126   potted plants, and continuous image sequences are extracted from the raw videos. To address motion blur that

127   may occur under handheld recording and non-ideal illumination conditions, the workflow introduces frequency-

128   domain image quality filtering to remove low-quality frames, thereby improving the stability of subsequent

129   feature matching and camera pose estimation. Then, a plant segmentation module is built based on vision

130   foundation models. It obtains plant foreground masks through text prompts and temporal propagation, and

131   separates complex backgrounds, pots, supports, and other non-target regions from multi-view images.

132      In the three-dimensional reconstruction stage, the preprocessed multi-view images first enter the SfM

133   pipeline to estimate camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters as well as sparse geometric structures. The

134   workflow then adopts 2D Gaussian Splatting to construct the three-dimensional plant representation, which

135   better matches the surface structure of thin sheet-like plant organs such as leaves. After the optimization of the

136   Gaussian representation, spatial artifact filtering and TSDF mesh extraction are further performed to convert

137   the three-dimensional representation into an explicit mesh model that can be directly measured. Finally, scale

138   recovery is completed using known scale markers in the scene, and basic phenotypic traits, including plant

139   height, canopy width, leaf length, and leaf width, are extracted from the scale-recovered model. The automatic

140   measurements are compared with manual measurements to evaluate the accuracy and practicality of the

141   workflow for three-dimensional plant phenotyping.

142      Fig. 1 shows the overall technical pipeline of F2DMAS. Compared with traditional three-dimensional plant

143   phenotyping pipelines that rely on dedicated hardware or substantial manual post-processing, F2DMAS

144   places greater emphasis on low-cost acquisition, automated processing, and continuity in the phenotypic

145   measurement pipeline. The focus of this method is not to propose a single low-level reconstruction algorithm,

146   but to integrate image quality control, plant segmentation, three-dimensional reconstruction, meshing, and

147   phenotypic measurement into a deployable application-oriented system.

151   2.2. Experimental Materials and Data Acquisition


152      This study constructs a multi-view RGB image dataset for three-dimensional plant phenotyping. Data are

153   acquired in two modes, namely fixed-device-assisted acquisition and handheld acquisition under complex

154   backgrounds. Table.1 summarizes the plant materials used in this study. The dataset contains 15 plant species

155   with different leaf morphologies, canopy structures, and background interference conditions, and is used to

156   evaluate the applicability of the F2DMAS workflow across diverse plant types and non-ideal acquisition

157   environments. Both acquisition modes use a consumer-grade smartphone for video recording and do not rely

158   on dedicated three-dimensional acquisition devices such as LiDAR, RGB-D cameras, or multi-camera arrays.

159      For fixed-device-assisted acquisition, this study builds a low-cost and portable plant video acquisition device,

160   as shown in Fig.2(a). The device consists of an electric turntable, a black background cloth, a smartphone, and

161   scale markers. During acquisition, the target potted plant is placed at the center of the electric turntable so that

162   the plant rotates slowly with the turntable, while the smartphone remains at a relatively fixed position and

163   continuously records video. Compared with handheld or tripod-based recording around the plant, this setting

164   obtains more stable multi-view image sequences in a smaller space, and is suitable for phenotyping in seedling
                                                                     7
165   rooms, laboratory benches, and indoor potted plant experiments. The background cloth reduces interference

166   from irrelevant objects in the environment. During acquisition, wrinkles on the cloth and natural shadows are

167   retained, so the images still contain a certain degree of non-uniform illumination, reflection, and texture

168   disturbance. This design allows the stability of plant segmentation and reconstruction to be evaluated under

169   backgrounds that are not fully controlled.

170      For complex-background acquisition, plants are placed in ordinary indoor environments or greenhouse-like

171   scenes. The background includes tabletops, supports, pots, walls, floors, and cluttered objects with colors

172   similar to the plant, as shown in Fig.2(b). The operator holds the smartphone and records video while moving

173   around the target plant, keeping the plant near the image center as much as possible and maintaining a relatively

174   stable field of view for the target plant in most video frames. This acquisition mode does not impose strict

175   constraints on camera height or shooting angle, and is closer to practical scenarios in which smartphones are

176   used for rapid recording of potted plants. It is used to evaluate the adaptability of the workflow to background

177   clutter, handheld motion, viewpoint changes, and illumination fluctuations.

178      All videos are captured using a consumer-grade iPhone 14 Pro Max smartphone, with a video resolution of

179   1080 x 1920, a frame rate of 60 FPS, and lens parameters of 13~mm and f/2.2. To improve the structural

180   completeness of three-dimensional reconstruction, each target plant is recorded using a two-height circular

181   acquisition strategy. During acquisition, the plant is first recorded from a lower viewpoint for one or more

182   circular passes to capture side-view information and the outer canopy contour. A second circular pass is then

183   recorded from a higher viewpoint with a certain downward angle to supplement information from the canopy

184   top and overlapping leaf regions. For fixed-device-assisted acquisition, the turntable completes an

185   approximately 360 rotation at each height. For handheld acquisition, the operator completes a continuous

186   circular recording of approximately 360 around the target plant. During acquisition, the plant is kept fully visible

187   in the image as much as possible, and severe motion blur caused by excessively fast movement is avoided.

188      To recover the scale of the three-dimensional model, scale markers or calibration references with known

189   dimensions are placed in the acquisition scene. In fixed-device-assisted acquisition, a black calibration card and

190   a red scale marker are placed near the target plant for subsequent model scaling and conversion to real-world
                                                               8
191   units. In complex-background acquisition, identifiable scale references are placed according to scene conditions,

192   so that the reconstructed model can be converted from relative coordinates to centimeter-scale measurements.

193   After acquisition, the raw videos are extracted into multi-view image sequences and then fed into the subsequent

194   pipeline, including frame quality filtering, plant segmentation, SfM pose estimation, 2DGS reconstruction, and

195   phenotypic measurement.

209   2.3. Frequency-Domain Frame Quality Screening


210      In video frame-based multi-view plant reconstruction, image sharpness affects feature matching, camera

211   pose estimation, and the stability of subsequent three-dimensional reconstruction(Zhu et al., 2023). In this study,

212   videos are captured using a handheld smartphone or a turntable-assisted setup, and the raw video sequences

213   may contain motion-blurred frames caused by camera shake, slight plant motion, or turntable rotation. If these

214   low-quality images are directly used in SfM and 2DGS reconstruction, they may lead to unstable feature

215   matching, increased camera pose estimation errors, and further degradation in model completeness and

216   phenotypic measurement. Therefore, F2DMAS introduces frequency-domain frame quality filtering during

217   image preprocessing to remove severely blurred frames and improve the reliability of subsequent multi-view

218   geometric computation. As shown in Fig.3, the frequency-spatial preprocessing stage consists of frequency-

219   domain frame quality screening and plant foreground extraction. The former removes severely blurred frames

220   according to high-frequency energy, while the latter uses prompt-guided plant segmentation, temporal mask

221   propagation, and morphological post-processing to generate clean plant foreground images for subsequent SfM

222   and 2DGS reconstruction.

251   2.4. Plant Segmentation Based on Vision Foundation Models


252      In unstructured or semi-structured plant acquisition scenes, raw images usually contain pots, soil, supports,

253   tabletops, background walls, and other interfering objects with colors similar to the plant. If the complete images

254   are directly fed into the three-dimensional reconstruction pipeline, background regions may participate in

255   camera matching and Gaussian representation optimization, leading to background adhesion, floating artifacts,
                                                                13
256   and redundant structures in the reconstructed model. This also increases the difficulty of subsequent mesh

257   cleaning and phenotypic measurement. Therefore, F2DMAS introduces a plant segmentation module before

258   three-dimensional reconstruction to extract the target plant region from multi-view images and reduce the

259   influence of complex backgrounds on the reconstruction results.

260      The FSAM3 module constructed in this paper uses the zero-shot segmentation ability of vision foundation

261   models and the temporal propagation mechanism in video sequences to extract the plant body. This design is

262   motivated by recent promptable segmentation foundation models. Segment Anything introduced a general

263   promptable segmentation framework with strong zero-shot transfer ability (Kirillov et al., 2023), while SAM 2

264   extended promptable segmentation from still images to videos through a streaming-memory mechanism (Ravi

265   et al., 2024). SAM 3 further introduces concept-prompt-based detection, segmentation, and tracking in images

266   and videos, which is consistent with the need to identify plant instances using semantic prompts in multi-view

267   video sequences (Carion et al., 2025). In addition, SEEM provides a promptable segmentation baseline that

268   supports multiple forms of user prompts, including language prompts (Zou et al., 2023).Unlike supervised

269   segmentation methods that depend on a large amount of pixel-level annotated data, this module uses text

270   prompts to guide the model in identifying the target plant region, without retraining the segmentation model for

271   different species or acquisition scenes. In the specific processing procedure, the system inputs a text prompt

272   that describes the target region, such as complete plant body without pot and background, to guide the model

273   in generating an initial mask of the target plant in the first frame or key frames. The model then uses temporal

274   information and memory propagation in the video sequence to continuously track and update the plant mask

275   across adjacent views, thereby reducing frame-by-frame manual interaction and alleviating inconsistency in

276   single-frame segmentation results.

311   2.5. 2DGS-Based Plant Three-Dimensional Reconstruction


312      After frequency-domain frame quality filtering and plant segmentation, F2DMAS feeds the processed multi-

313   view plant foreground images into the three-dimensional reconstruction module. This module first uses

314   Structure from Motion (SfM) to estimate camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters as well as sparse point cloud

315   structures, providing camera poses and initial geometric information for subsequent 2D Gaussian Splatting

316   (2DGS) reconstruction. Compared with direct reconstruction of complete complex scenes, the images

317   preprocessed by FSAM3 reduce interference from pots, supports, background walls, and other non-target

318   regions, which helps focus the reconstruction process on the plant body structure.



                                                                       16
319      Plant leaves, petioles, and canopy boundaries usually exhibit thin sheet-like and surface-dominant geometric

320   characteristics. Traditional 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) represents a scene using three-dimensional

321   volumetric Gaussian primitives. When it handles thin structures such as leaves, it may produce surface

322   thickening, boundary expansion, and adhesion in overlapping regions, thereby affecting subsequent explicit

323   mesh extraction and phenotypic measurement. In contrast, 2DGS describes scene structures using two-

324   dimensional Gaussian primitives oriented along local tangent planes, which is more suitable for representing

325   surface-like organs such as plant leaves. Therefore, this paper adopts 2DGS as the main three-dimensional

326   representation in the F2DMAS workflow to improve the reconstruction stability of thin plant structures and

327   provide depth and normal information for subsequent meshing.

328      In the 2DGS representation, each Gaussian primitive can be regarded as a local two-dimensional surface

329   patch in three-dimensional space. The primitive consists of parameters such as a three-dimensional center

330   position, local two-dimensional scale, rotation matrix, color, and opacity. The rotation matrix defines the

331   orientation of the local tangent plane, the scale parameters control the spatial extent of the Gaussian primitive

332   on the local surface, and the opacity parameter represents the contribution of the primitive to the final rendered

333   image. Given camera poses and multi-view images, 2DGS optimizes these Gaussian parameters through

334   differentiable rendering so that the rendered results remain consistent with the input images. Its local two-

335   dimensional Gaussian distribution is expressed as:

337      where 𝑢 denotes the two-dimensional coordinate on the local tangent plane, and 𝑆 denotes the two-

338   dimensional scale matrix. Unlike volumetric Gaussian representations that perform integration along camera

339   rays, 2DGS focuses more on the geometric relationship between camera rays and local surface patches, and can

340   therefore generate depth maps and normal maps related to plant leaf surfaces.

341      During training, the system optimizes 2DGS using plant foreground images from different views based on

342   the camera parameters estimated by SfM. The optimization objective mainly includes image reconstruction

343   errors and structural consistency constraints, so that the Gaussian representation can stably reproduce the


                                                             17
344   appearance and geometry of the plant across multiple views. After training, the 2DGS model can not only render

345   plant images from different viewpoints, but also output corresponding depth maps and normal maps. These

346   geometric cues are further used for TSDF mesh fusion, which converts the renderable Gaussian representation

347   into an explicit three-dimensional mesh that can be directly measured.

348      It should be noted that this paper does not propose 2DGS as a new low-level reconstruction algorithm.

349   Instead, it uses 2DGS as the core three-dimensional representation module in the F2DMAS workflow. The main

350   reason for choosing 2DGS is that its surface-oriented representation is consistent with the structural

351   characteristics of thin sheet-like organs such as plant leaves, while its depth and normal outputs facilitate

352   subsequent mesh extraction and scale-aware phenotypic measurement. By combining FSAM3 preprocessing,

353   SfM camera estimation, 2DGS representation, and explicit meshing, F2DMAS generates plant three-

354   dimensional models suitable for phenotypic analysis from consumer-grade smartphone videos.

364   2.6. Joint Depth-Normal Explicit Meshing


365      After 2DGS optimization, the system obtains a renderable Gaussian scene representation. This representation

366   can generate multi-view images, depth maps, and normal maps, but plant phenotypic measurement usually

367   requires a measurable explicit three-dimensional geometric model. Therefore, after reconstruction, F2DMAS

368   further performs spatial artifact cleaning and explicit mesh extraction to convert the 2DGS representation into

369   a triangular mesh model for scale recovery and phenotypic measurement.

370      Under complex backgrounds and multi-view occlusion, a small number of low-contribution or locally

371   abnormal Gaussian primitives may be produced during 2DGS optimization. These artifacts usually appear near

372   leaf occlusion boundaries, regions with insufficient view coverage, or residual background areas, and are

373   observed as dark patches, floating points, or isolated fragments. To reduce their influence on subsequent

374   meshing and phenotypic measurement, this paper adopts a lightweight spatial cleaning strategy before mesh

375   extraction. Specifically, the system extracts the base color components of Gaussian primitives and identifies

376   low-contribution dark artifacts according to perceptual luminance. Gaussian primitives whose luminance is

377   lower than a predefined threshold and whose contribution to the plant body structure is limited are removed

378   during post-processing. This step does not change the basic optimization process of 2DGS, and mainly reduces

379   floating artifacts and local noise before meshing.

448   stability of F2DMAS across different phenotypic traits. This validation process evaluates whether the complete

449   workflow, from smartphone video acquisition, three-dimensional reconstruction, and scale recovery to

452   oriented scenarios, and therefore selects plant height, canopy width, leaf length, and leaf width as the main

453   validation traits. These traits have clear manual measurement references and can reflect the geometric reliability

454   of the reconstructed model at both the overall canopy scale and the local leaf scale. More complex phenotypic

455   traits, such as leaf area, leaf inclination angle, canopy volume, and plant architecture compactness, can be

456   further extended based on the scale-recovered mesh.

586   maintains good continuity along leaf margins, canopy contours, and fine structures. For images with leaf

                                                                     28

662      To evaluate the performance of F2DMAS in 3D plant reconstruction, this study compares it with

663   COLMAP, standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), and the standard 2DGS pipeline. COLMAP serves as a

664   representative traditional SfM/MVS multi-view geometric reconstruction pipeline. 3DGS represents an

665   explicit neural rendering method based on 3D Gaussian primitives, while standard 2DGS is used to analyze

666   the surface Gaussian reconstruction performance without the complete preprocessing pipeline of F2DMAS.

667   Through these comparisons, the reconstruction quality and processing efficiency of F2DMAS in plant 3D

668   phenotyping scenarios are evaluated from the perspectives of traditional photogrammetry, 3D Gaussian

669   representation, and 2D surface Gaussian representation.

670      Table.5 reports the rendering quality and processing time of different reconstruction pipelines on test

671   views. Compared with COLMAP, F2DMAS achieves better results on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. COLMAP

672   obtains a PSNR of 13.63 dB, an SSIM of 0.8745, and an LPIPS of 0.1072, whereas F2DMAS achieves a

673   PSNR of 31.09 dB, an SSIM of 0.9711, and an LPIPS of 0.0365. These results indicate that, under complex

674   plant structures and non-ideal background conditions, it is difficult to obtain stable view reconstruction

675   quality by relying only on traditional sparse and dense geometric reconstruction pipelines.

676      Compared with 3DGS, F2DMAS maintains high rendering quality while substantially reducing mesh

677   extraction time. The PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS of 3DGS are 30.17 dB, 0.9587, and 0.0386, respectively, while

678   F2DMAS achieves 31.09 dB, 0.9711, and 0.0365. In terms of processing efficiency, the mesh extraction time

679   of 3DGS is 642 s, whereas F2DMAS requires only 55 s, corresponding to a reduction of approximately

680   91.4%. This result shows that, in plant phenotypic measurement tasks, F2DMAS not only provides stable


                                                                33
681   visual reconstruction results but also generates explicit mesh models for subsequent measurement more

682   efficiently.

683      The comparison with standard 2DGS further shows that the performance gains of F2DMAS mainly come

684   from the optimization of input data by front-end quality filtering and plant instance segmentation. Standard

685   2DGS obtains a PSNR of 29.58 dB, an SSIM of 0.9574, and an LPIPS of 0.0487, with training time and mesh

686   extraction time of 12,913.7 s and 157.9 s, respectively. F2DMAS increases the PSNR to 31.09 dB and reduces

687   the training time and mesh extraction time to 5,044.5 s and 55 s, respectively. These results indicate that, for

688   plant images captured in complex backgrounds, removing severely blurred frames and separating the plant

689   instance before reconstruction can reduce the interference of background regions in Gaussian representation

690   optimization and mesh extraction, thereby improving the overall efficiency and usability of the workflow.

693   3.5. Workflow Ablation Study


694      To analyze the effects of different preprocessing modules in F2DMAS on reconstruction results, this study

695   designs a workflow ablation experiment. The standard 2DGS pipeline is used as the Base setting. The

696   frequency-domain frame quality filtering module and the plant instance segmentation module are then added

697   separately to form four settings, namely Base, Base+FFT, Base+SAM3, and the full F2DMAS workflow. By

698   comparing PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, training time, and mesh extraction time under different settings, this

699   experiment evaluates the roles of image quality filtering and plant foreground segmentation in the 3D

700   reconstruction pipeline.

                                                                       34
701      Table.6 reports the ablation results. Compared with Base, adding FFT-based frequency-domain frame

702   quality filtering increases PSNR from 29.58 dB to 29.80 dB, increases SSIM from 0.9574 to 0.9623, and

703   decreases LPIPS from 0.0487 to 0.0453. The training time and mesh extraction time also decrease from

704   12,913.7 s and 157.9 s to 12,510.3 s and 144.3 s, respectively. These results indicate that removing severely

705   blurred frames improves the quality of input images to some extent and reduces the influence of low-quality

706   views on feature matching and Gaussian optimization. However, the improvement brought by frame quality

707   filtering alone remains limited.

708      After introducing the plant instance segmentation module, the improvements become more evident.

709   Base+SAM3 increases PSNR to 30.50 dB and SSIM to 0.9687, while reducing LPIPS to 0.0397. The training

710   time decreases from 12,913.7 s in Base to 6,541.3 s, and the mesh extraction time decreases from 157.9 s to

711   73.6 s. These results show that, for plant images captured in complex backgrounds, background regions

712   increase the computational cost of Gaussian representation optimization and mesh extraction. By separating

713   the plant instance from the background, the segmentation module allows the reconstruction process to focus

714   more on the target plant region, thereby improving both reconstruction quality and processing efficiency.

715      The full F2DMAS workflow introduces both frequency-domain frame quality filtering and plant instance

716   segmentation, and achieves the best results across all metrics. Its PSNR reaches 31.09 dB, SSIM reaches

717   0.9711, and LPIPS decreases to 0.0365. The training time and mesh extraction time decrease to 5,044.5 s and

718   55.0 s, respectively. Compared with Base, the full workflow reduces training time by 60.94% and mesh

719   extraction time by 65.17%. These results indicate that frequency-domain quality filtering and spatial-domain

720   plant instance segmentation are complementary. The former mainly reduces the influence of blurred frames

721   on multi-view geometric estimation and reconstruction optimization, while the latter mainly reduces the

722   interference of background regions in Gaussian representation and meshing. When combined, these two

723   modules improve both reconstruction stability and processing efficiency of F2DMAS under non-ideal

724   acquisition conditions.

727     3.6. Mesh Quality Analysis in Overlapping Leaf Regions


728         In addition to rendering metrics and processing time, 3D plant phenotyping also requires attention to

729     whether the reconstructed model preserves geometric structures that are useful for measurement. For potted

730     plants and seedling canopies, leaf occlusion and overlap are important factors that affect 3D phenotypic

731     measurement. If the reconstructed mesh shows adhesion, blurred boundaries, or local surface swelling in

732     overlapping leaf regions, the extraction of phenotypic traits such as leaf length, leaf width, canopy width, and

733     canopy structure is directly affected. Therefore, this study further conducts a qualitative comparison of plant

734     meshes generated by different methods, with emphasis on geometric recovery in overlapping leaf regions.

735         As shown in Fig.7, the target plant in the original image contains clear leaf overlap and canopy occlusion.

736     The mesh generated by SuGaR recovers the overall morphology of the plant instance, but boundary blurring

737     and local adhesion tend to appear in leaf intersection regions, and the spatial separation between adjacent

738     leaves is not sufficiently clear. These problems affect subsequent leaf-level phenotypic measurement. In

739     particular, when identifying leaf boundaries or extracting local leaf width, adhesive regions may lead to

740     overestimated measurements or reduced measurement stability.

741         In contrast, the mesh generated by F2DMAS is more concentrated on the plant instance region, with fewer

742     background residues and isolated fragments. In overlapping leaf regions, F2DMAS preserves the boundary

743     relationships between adjacent leaves more clearly and reduces abnormal adhesion between leaves, making

744     the canopy structure and major leaf contours more suitable for subsequent virtual measurement. This result

745     indicates that the combination of FSAM3-based foreground decoupling and 2DGS surface representation not
                                                               36
746   only improves rendering quality but also enhances the usability of explicit meshes for plant phenotypic

747   measurement.

748      It should be noted that F2DMAS does not fully solve fine-grained leaf geometry recovery. In very thin leaf

749   margins, oblique-view regions, and severely occluded areas, the mesh may still show local boundary shifts or

750   slight surface swelling. This observation is consistent with the relatively higher error and positive bias

751   observed in subsequent leaf width measurement. Therefore, this study positions F2DMAS as a deployable

752   workflow for 3D plant phenotyping. Boundary correction and topology optimization for thin leaf structures

753   remain important directions for future methodological research.

791     4. Discussion


792     4.1. Application Significance of F2DMAS for Low-Cost 3D Plant Phenotyping


793         F2DMAS proposed in this study is not an isolated algorithm designed only to optimize reconstruction

794     metrics. Instead, it is a complete workflow that covers smartphone video acquisition, 3D model generation,

795     and phenotypic trait output. In greenhouse seedling cultivation, potted-plant experiments, and indoor plant

796     phenotyping studies, the acquisition of 3D structural data is often limited by sensing equipment,

797     environmental conditions, and post-processing cost. LiDAR, multi-camera systems, and some RGB-D devices

798     can provide high-quality 3D data, but their hardware cost, acquisition configuration, and data processing

799     requirements remain relatively high. As a result, they are difficult to deploy widely in small- and medium-

800     scale breeding trials, teaching and research activities, and routine plant monitoring. In contrast, F2DMAS only

801     relies on consumer-grade smartphones to capture multi-view RGB videos and does not require dedicated 3D

802     sensors or complex acquisition platforms, thereby lowering the practical barrier to 3D plant phenotyping from

803     the data acquisition stage.

804         From the perspective of the application workflow, the role of F2DMAS is not limited to generating plant

805     3D models, but also lies in converting the models into quantifiable phenotypic data. Traditional 3D

806     reconstruction pipelines often take model visualization as the main output, while subsequent steps still require


                                                                  41
807   manual background cleaning, model repair, scale setting, and trait measurement. F2DMAS connects video

808   frame extraction, image quality filtering, plant instance segmentation, camera pose estimation, 2DGS

809   reconstruction, mesh extraction, scale recovery, and phenotypic measurement into a continuous process from

810   acquisition to reconstruction, measurement, and result output. The experimental results show that this

811   workflow outputs basic phenotypic traits such as plant height, canopy width, leaf length, and leaf width, and

812   maintains high consistency with manual measurements. This indicates that the goal of F2DMAS is not simply

813   to obtain reconstructed images with higher PSNR, but to provide an executable 3D data production workflow

814   for agricultural research and plant phenotypic analysis.

815      For practical users, this workflow is easy to understand and execute. In a typical greenhouse seedling or

816   potted-plant experiment, researchers can use a smartphone to capture a dual-height surrounding video of a

817   single plant for 1--2 min and upload the video to a computing server. The system then automatically performs

818   frame filtering, plant segmentation, 3D reconstruction, mesh generation, and phenotypic measurement. The

819   final outputs include a scale-aware 3D mesh model, visualization results, and a phenotypic trait table. This

820   mode is suitable for small- and medium-scale breeding trials, controlled-environment plant experiments,

821   teaching demonstrations, and the accumulation of 3D plant phenotypic data.


822   4.2. Role of Frequency- and Spatial-Domain Preprocessing in Deployability


823      Under non-ideal acquisition conditions, input image quality and plant instance segmentation directly affect

824   subsequent 3D reconstruction. Smartphone video acquisition lowers the hardware barrier, but it also

825   introduces hand motion, motion blur, exposure variation, and background clutter. If low-quality images and

826   complex backgrounds are directly fed into SfM or 2DGS, they may lead to unstable camera pose estimation,

827   background participation in Gaussian optimization, and floating points or background adhesion in the

828   resulting mesh. Therefore, front-end preprocessing is not a simple image enhancement step, but a key

829   component that determines whether the workflow can run automatically and stably.

830      The frequency-domain frame quality filtering module in F2DMAS is mainly used to remove frames with

831   severe motion blur. Although this step is lightweight, it has a clear role in smartphone-based acquisition
                                                             42
832   scenarios. It reduces the interference of low-quality views in feature matching and camera registration, and

833   provides a more stable input sequence for subsequent SfM and 2DGS. The ablation study also shows that

834   adding FFT-based filtering alone improves both reconstruction quality and processing time to some extent.

835   This indicates that, in a workflow designed for practical deployment, even a relatively simple quality control

836   step can contribute positively to overall stability.

837      Compared with frequency-domain filtering, plant instance segmentation has a more substantial effect on

838   workflow efficiency and result quality. In complex backgrounds, non-target regions such as pots, soil,

839   supports, tabletops, and walls occupy Gaussian representation capacity and increase the difficulty of mesh

840   post-processing. FSAM3 combines the zero-shot segmentation capability of a visual foundation model with

841   temporal propagation in video sequences, producing relatively stable plant foreground masks without relying

842   on manual annotations. Compared with single-frame segmentation, temporal propagation helps maintain

843   target-region consistency across multi-view sequences and reduces reconstruction instability caused by abrupt

844   mask changes between adjacent views. The segmentation results not only outperform SEEM in F1-score,

845   mIoU, and HD95, but more importantly provide cleaner plant-instance inputs for subsequent 3D

846   reconstruction, reducing the probability that background regions enter the 2DGS optimization and TSDF

847   meshing processes.

848      Therefore, the role of FSAM3 in F2DMAS should not be understood only as improving segmentation

849   accuracy. It should be viewed as a front-end input purification mechanism for deployable 3D reconstruction.

850   This mechanism reduces the cost of manual background cleaning and makes multi-view plant images more

851   suitable for automated reconstruction. This is also one of the main advantages of F2DMAS over directly using

852   standard 2DGS.


853   4.3. Applicability of 2DGS to Plant Surface Reconstruction


854      The requirements of 3D plant phenotypic measurement differ from those of conventional novel view

855   synthesis. For phenotypic analysis, the model needs not only good visual realism, but also geometric

856   structures that can support measurement, such as canopy outlines, leaf boundaries, spatial separation between
                                                             43
857   leaves, and the overall plant scale. Plant leaves usually have thin-sheet structures and surface-dominant

858   geometry, which are closer to 2D surfaces than to volumetric structures. Therefore, whether a 3D

859   representation can effectively describe thin leaf structures directly affects subsequent mesh extraction and

860   phenotypic measurement.

861      3DGS represents a scene using 3D ellipsoidal Gaussian primitives and provides high rendering efficiency.

862   However, surface thickening and adhesion between adjacent structures tend to occur near leaf boundaries,

863   occluded regions, and thin-sheet structures. For tasks mainly focused on image rendering, such local

864   geometric errors may not be obvious. In phenotypic measurement tasks such as leaf length, leaf width, and

865   canopy width estimation, however, boundary expansion and leaf adhesion directly affect the measurement

866   results. In contrast, 2DGS uses 2D Gaussian primitives oriented to local surfaces, which better match the

867   geometry of thin-sheet organs such as leaves. It also outputs depth maps and normal maps, providing more

868   suitable geometric information for subsequent TSDF meshing.

869      The mesh visualization results in this study also support this analysis. In overlapping leaf regions, SuGaR

870   or reconstructions based on volumetric Gaussians tend to show boundary blurring and local adhesion, while

871   the mesh generated by F2DMAS preserves the separation between adjacent leaves more clearly. This

872   difference has a direct impact on plant phenotypic measurement. Whether leaves remain relatively

873   independent in the mesh affects the virtual extraction of traits such as leaf length, leaf width, and canopy

874   structure. Therefore, F2DMAS selects 2DGS not simply to pursue better rendering metrics, but to obtain a 3D

875   representation that is more suitable for explicit meshing and phenotypic measurement.

876      At the same time, 2DGS does not fully solve all problems in plant geometry reconstruction. For fine leaf

877   margins, heavily occluded regions, and leaf boundaries under oblique views, the current mesh may still show

878   local expansion or boundary shifts. This problem is evident in leaf width measurement, where the virtual

879   measurements show the lowest R² among the four traits, together with a MAPE of 9.73% and a positive bias

880   of 0.38 cm. This observation indicates that F2DMAS can already support canopy-scale and partial leaf-scale

881   phenotypic measurement, but there remains room for further optimization in high-precision local leaf

882   geometry recovery.

884   4.4. Deployment Pathway for Agricultural Research Scenarios


885      The potential application scenarios of F2DMAS mainly include greenhouse seedling cultivation, potted-

886   plant experiments, controlled-environment phenotyping studies, and teaching and research platforms. Unlike

887   large-scale field high-throughput phenotyping platforms, these scenarios usually have moderate sample sizes

888   and limited acquisition space, but are more sensitive to equipment cost, operational complexity, and data

889   processing efficiency. Researchers need to obtain 3D models and basic phenotypic traits of individual plants

890   at relatively low cost, while avoiding complex hardware setup and extensive manual post-processing.

891      In practice, F2DMAS can adopt a cloud-edge collaborative deployment mode. The user side mainly

892   performs low-cost data acquisition, using a smartphone to record multi-view videos around the target plant.

893   The computing side performs image filtering, plant segmentation, camera pose estimation, 2DGS

894   reconstruction, mesh extraction, and phenotypic measurement. This mode avoids the need for high-

895   performance GPUs on the user side while retaining the flexibility of smartphone acquisition. For breeding

896   researchers, the final system output is not only a single 3D visualization model, but also a phenotypic trait

897   table suitable for statistical analysis, including basic geometric traits such as plant height, canopy width, leaf

898   length, and leaf width.

899      This deployment mode helps transform a complex computer vision pipeline into a data acquisition tool that

900   is easier for agricultural users to operate. In small-scale breeding trials, teaching experiments, and dynamic

901   observations of plant growth, researchers can periodically collect videos of the same batch of potted plants

902   and use F2DMAS to generate comparable 3D phenotypic records. Although the current study does not yet

903   cover real field scenarios or continuous full-life-cycle monitoring, the combination of smartphone acquisition

904   and automated processing provides a feasible pathway for low-cost 3D plant phenotypic data acquisition.

                                                               45
906   4.5. Limitations and Future Work


907      Although F2DMAS achieves good reconstruction quality, mesh extraction efficiency, and phenotypic

908   measurement consistency on the dataset used in this study, several limitations remain. First, the current data

909   mainly come from potted plants, fixed-device-assisted acquisition, and complex indoor-background

910   acquisition, and do not yet cover real field scenarios. Field environments involve stronger natural illumination

911   variation, wind-induced plant motion, complex soil backgrounds, and multi-plant occlusion, which may have

912   greater effects on image segmentation, camera pose estimation, and 3D reconstruction. Therefore, the stability

913   of the workflow still needs to be further verified in greenhouse production environments and real field

914   conditions.

915      Second, the current dataset does not cover the complete growth period of plants, and mainly verifies 3D

916   reconstruction and basic phenotypic measurement under single-acquisition conditions. For plant growth

917   analysis, stress response monitoring, and breeding selection, the consistency of 3D phenotypes across time

918   series is also important. Future studies can perform continuous acquisition at different growth stages of the

919   same plants to evaluate the repeatability and sensitivity of F2DMAS in temporal phenotypic analysis.

920      Third, the current phenotypic traits mainly focus on basic geometric traits such as plant height, canopy

921   width, leaf length, and leaf width. These traits have clear manual measurement references and are suitable for

922   validating the basic measurement capability of the workflow, but they are not sufficient to cover all needs in

923   plant phenotyping research. In the future, scale-aware meshes can be further used to extract 3D phenotypic

924   traits such as leaf area, canopy volume, leaf inclination angle, plant compactness, and stem morphology, and

925   to explore their relationships with biomass, growth status, and cultivar differences.

926      Fourth, FSAM3 still relies on text prompts and the generalization capability of visual foundation models.

927   When the background is highly complex, the plant and background have very similar colors, or the plant is

928   severely occluded, a single prompt may be insufficient to obtain stable masks. Future work can further

929   investigate prompt ensembling, lightweight domain adaptation, and few-shot fine-tuning strategies to improve

930   the stability of plant instance segmentation across more agricultural scenarios.

                                                             46
931      Finally, F2DMAS still requires relatively high computational resources, and the inference of visual

932   foundation models and 2DGS optimization are more suitable for cloud servers or workstations. Therefore,

933   future work can optimize the overall efficiency through model lightweighting, parallel task scheduling, and

934   batch processing system design, making the workflow more suitable for medium- and large-scale plant

935   phenotypic data production. Meanwhile, to address boundary expansion, positive bias, and relatively high

936   MAPE in leaf width measurement, future research further explores plant-aware Gaussian optimization,

937   topology-aware pruning, and edge-aware meshing to improve the accuracy of fine-grained leaf phenotypic

938   measurement.


939   5. Conclusion


940      This study proposes F2DMAS, a smartphone-based 3D plant phenotyping workflow for unstructured

941   acquisition environments. The workflow takes multi-view RGB videos captured by consumer-grade

942   smartphones as input and sequentially performs frame quality filtering, plant instance segmentation, SfM

943   camera pose estimation, 2D Gaussian Splatting-based 3D reconstruction, spatial artifact removal, TSDF mesh

944   extraction, scale recovery, and phenotypic trait measurement. It enables automated processing from

945   smartphone video acquisition to the output of scale-aware 3D models and phenotypic data. Compared with

946   traditional pipelines that rely on dedicated 3D sensors or extensive manual post-processing, F2DMAS

947   emphasizes low-cost acquisition, automated processing, and deployability for phenotypic measurement.

948      The experimental results show that F2DMAS provides stable plant instance segmentation and 3D

949   reconstruction results for plant images captured in complex backgrounds. The front-end FSAM3 segmentation

950   module achieves an F1-score of 98.3% and an mIoU of 97.9%, with a markedly lower HD95 than SEEM,

951   indicating that it extracts the plant instance region more completely. In terms of reconstruction quality and

952   processing efficiency, compared with standard 2DGS, F2DMAS improves PSNR and SSIM by 5.10% and

953   1.43%, respectively, and reduces LPIPS by 25.05%. Meanwhile, it reduces training time and mesh extraction

954   time by 60.94% and 65.17%, respectively. Compared with 3DGS, F2DMAS reduces mesh extraction time by

955   91.4%, showing higher processing efficiency for explicit mesh generation and batch phenotyping scenarios.
                                                             47
956      For phenotypic measurement, F2DMAS extracts basic traits such as plant height, canopy width, leaf

957   length, and leaf width from scale-recovered 3D meshes. Plant height, canopy width, and leaf length achieve

958   high agreement with manual measurements, with R² values of 0.9878, 0.9879, and 0.9738, respectively. Leaf

959   width shows a lower R² of 0.8999 and the highest MAPE of 9.73%, indicating that fine-grained leaf-width

960   measurement remains more sensitive to local boundary reconstruction and meshing errors. Overall, the

961   workflow reliably supports basic 3D plant phenotypic analysis, especially for canopy-scale traits, while thin-

962   leaf boundary correction remains an important direction for further improvement.

963      Overall, F2DMAS provides a low-cost and automated solution for 3D plant phenotyping in greenhouse

964   seedling cultivation, potted-plant experiments, controlled-environment phenotyping studies, and teaching and

965   research scenarios. Future work further extends the workflow to real field scenarios and full-growth-period

966   validation, and improves fine-grained leaf geometry reconstruction and phenotypic measurement accuracy

967   through plant-aware Gaussian optimization, topology-aware pruning, and edge-aware meshing.
